# Home room

The home page's **Cabin** (issue #14, ADR-0011 and ADR-0012), which replaced the planned 3D room of issue #13. This note holds what was measured while prototyping on 2026-11-11 and while building the Cabin, and the traps found on the way. Each number says where it came from.

## The reference: felixrieseberg.com

Read from its shipped HTML, CSS and scripts and from network traffic in a real browser.

- **Engine.** three.js r169 (`window.__THREE__` = `169`), WebGL2 on the canvas. A real-time scene, not video or pre-rendered frames.
- **Weight.** First load of the room: 88 requests, about 7.2 MB (sum of `encodedBodySize` over resource entries). Largest: packed geometry `leanm/geo/scene.json`, 3.9 MB; `three.module.js`, 260 KB. Scene assets are served from a separate Cloudflare Pages deploy (`berlin-monitor-scene.pages.dev`); the site itself is Ghost.
- **View control.** three.js `OrbitControls`, not a first-person look: target on the desk at (0.04, 1.1, 0.02), home distance 1.75 m, distance 0.3–2.6 m, azimuth −1.3…1.2 rad, polar 0.95…1.72 rad, `enableDamping` with `dampingFactor` 0.06, pan off, `rotateSpeed` 0.5. A `keepInRoom` function clamps the eye inside the room and out of furniture (`OBST`). Pages fly the camera to an object (`ANCHOR`, `frameFor`, `fly`) and pin their card beside it. At 1.75 m and 74° the eye moves about 1.7 m sideways, which is why the window shows a different part of the city from each angle; confirmed by dragging in the live site.
- **Living photo.** The window view is a photograph, `tex/berlin.jpg`, 3840×3201 (a Wikimedia Commons Berlin skyline). Per the header of `city/lib/living-shader.js`: a fitted camera model (f = 2451 px/rad, horizon at photo row 2090, photographer 12 m above the rails), a cylinder mapping (3840 / 2451 ≈ 1.57 rad ≈ 90° wide), a depth map `city/assets/depth.png` (1022×854) so masts and buildings "move as solid things when the camera does", and shader layers for aircraft, trains, weather and mist. Every animated value is a pure function of absolute time, so a tab hidden for an hour resumes in the right state.
- **Light and materials.** Baked lighting: in the main scene script `bake` appears 37 times, `baked` 24, `lightMap` 7, `aoMap` 4, `roughnessMap` 8, `normalMap` 2. Switching the desk lamp off loads "its own bake (`bake/*_irrD.jpg`) and its own panorama for reflections (`tex/env_off.jpg`)". Post: `AgXToneMapping`, `UnrealBloomPass`, `OutputPass`, `PMREMGenerator`. Whether it uses photographed material textures was not verified: no such images showed in the network list; they may be inside the packed geometry, which was not opened.
- **Loading.** Geometry is meshopt-compressed (`MeshoptDecoder`, `lean/`); a comment notes AVIF and fetching "what home doesn't show after the click" later.
- **Fallback.** Devices judged unsuitable are routed to a static reading page (`?reading=1`; `?room=1` forces the room). Its layout: content 660 px wide; h1 Literata 46/700, body Literata 21, h2 Fira Sans 22/700, metadata in Fira Code, wordmark in Archivo (computed styles, light theme).

## The robotaxi cabin reference

The scene concept moved to the inside of a two-seat robotaxi (no steering wheel, a larger screen). What the real cabin is, from five interior photos (topelectricsuv.com Paris 2026 and Auto Zurich 2026 show cars, a notateslaapp.com press render) and the text of those articles. "Seen" means read off a photo; quotes are article text.

- **Screen.** Freestanding on the centre of the dash, close to upright, roughly 4:3 landscape with a thin black bezel; its lower edge hangs below the dash top line. "a large, freestanding touchscreen" (topelectricsuv, both). Size is given as 20.5" (notateslaapp, 2024 prototype) or 21" (basenor); sources disagree.
- **Dash.** Seen: a thin horizontal stack across the full width: a deep flat top running far forward to the windscreen, a full-width slot with a light strip (lit warm in the render, a pale trim line in the show cars), a dark grey lower fascia. Below it the floor carpet climbs a near-vertical face: "a nearly vertical surface beneath the dashboard, which provides a convenient place to rest my feet" (topelectricsuv). No glovebox.
- **Seats.** Two, set close like a split bench: "broad, horizontally segmented cushions reminiscent of old-school American lounge chairs" (topelectricsuv). Seen: separate tall headrests, one horizontal seam across the backrest, two across the cushion, a floating centre armrest, two recessed cup holders in a console between the cushions in front of it. Show cars are dark grey leather; the render and the 2024 prototype ("only shown the white interior … with black trim", notateslaapp) are light.
- **Roof and overhead.** "There is no panoramic glass roof" (topelectricsuv). Seen: light grey headliner, an overhead pod at the top of the windscreen, two dome lights with a stop button between them ("A Stop button between the dome lights", topelectricsuv Zurich).
- **Doors and trim.** Dihedral doors (articles disagree on the word). Seen: light grey moulded door panels with a sculpted pull, wide light grey A-pillars, dark grey flat carpet over an open floor. Window switches: "two window switches located under the touchscreen".
- **Glass.** Tint is not stated anywhere; the render shows warm amber light through the glass, the show-car photos show clear glass.
- **Dimensions.** Width 69 in, height 55.4 in (topelectricsuv); headroom 38.3 in, legroom 43.4 in, U.S. spec (topelectricsuv Zurich). Length unpublished.
- **What v9 (2026-11-11) got wrong against this:** a wide flat screen on a stalk instead of an upright 4:3 one hanging in front of the dash; no light-strip slot; no carpeted footwell face; seats with no headrests and no seam pattern; amber glass the show cars do not have.

## The Cabin's shape

What `tools/cabin/cabin.py` models and what each dimension was read from, written with the reshape on 2026-11-12 (#19). Units are metres in Blender's axes (y forward, z up, z = height above the road). None of the reference images is in the repository.

- **References.** The maker's rider-guide interior image (a centred front view from behind the seats with numbered callouts: dome lights, stop button, cabin camera, climate vents, touchscreen, door lever, USB-C ports, window buttons) and its exterior side, front and rear views; preview renders of a third-party paid model on RenderHub, licensed for editorial use only, which were looked at and not downloaded or used (two are subdivision-level-0 wireframes that show how the surfaces flow); the show-car photos above. The rider-guide image decides what exists and where.
- **Heights, from the exterior side view.** The roof is 630 px above the ground there and the published height is 55.4 in (1.41 m), so 2.24 mm/px. Read off it: cowl at the windscreen base about 0.93, front door belt about 0.95, hood nose top about 0.64, hood about 1.03 long and nearly level for its rear half. The model uses dash top 0.92, belt 0.955, windscreen base 0.935; the prototype had the dash at 0.74 and the belt at 0.84, which is why it read as a tub with no car around it.
- **The screen against the dash, from the rider-guide image.** The screen is 240 px tall there (0.32 m); its top is about 70 px (9 cm) above the dash top and its bottom about 40 px (5 cm) below the slab. The model keeps the screen where the prototype had it (0.68–1.00, centre 0.84, 0.03 m in front of the dash lip), so the orbit target and the fitted orbit did not change; with the slab at 0.745–0.92 the screen's top sits 8 cm above and its bottom 6.5 cm below.
- **The cabin narrows toward the front.** In the rider-guide image the doors meet the dash 1,040 px apart against the screen's 370 px (0.43 m), about 1.2 m; the prototype was a straight 1.54 m box. `inset(y)` draws the sides in by 0.16 between y 0.35 and 1.0 (doors 1.22 apart at the dash). The door's belt shelf draws in only 40% of that, so the A-pillar bases stand 1.33 apart and the pillar tops lean out to the roof rails, 1.40 apart; the windscreen is wider at the top than the dash, as in the references.
- **The hood shows because the dash sits below the cowl's sightline.** From the page's opening eye (z 1.207) the line over the dash deck's front edge (z 0.957 at y 1.80) falls at slope −0.127, and the hood's level rear stretch (z 0.943 on the centre line at y 2.3) lies above it at −0.107, so a band of hood shows over the dash with the fender crowns rising at its corners. It was not visible in the prototype because there was no body at all.
- **The side glass is its own mesh, `Glass`,** with a faint grey tint at alpha 0.25 (Blender 5.3's glTF exporter writes it as `alphaMode: BLEND`). It is hidden during the bake, so the lightmaps are the cabin's light without glass, and the page leaves the lightmap off it.
- **Sweeps and lofts instead of boxes.** The windscreen's rim and the roof rails are bands between two edge curves (below); the dash slab is one sweep of its layer-stack profile (lip, upper layer, the vent slot with the light strip, lower layer, a stepped-back underside) whose ends turn rearward and sink into the doors; the door armrest is a ledge swept down from behind the dash end to the seat armrest height. Faces meeting at over 40° keep a crease (sharp edges) so a hard profile does not shade as a tube.
- **The pillar-to-headliner corners are one band, not a swept profile** (#20). Until #20 the windscreen frame was a 0.15 m wide profile swept along one centre line up the pillar, round the corner and across the header, with the roof rail butted against the corner. The centre line turned about 80° within 0.1 m while the profile's half-width there was 0.094 m, so the sweep folded over itself on the inside of the turn (the small protruding flap), the rail's capped end stood out beside it, and the headliner sheet's stepped edge cut through the rail. Now the rim is given as its two edges, the windscreen opening's and the one against the headliner and the side glass, with a rounded slab between each pair of points (`ribbon`): across the header the band hangs 4 cm below the headliner, round the corner the outer edge swings wide so the band fans out to about 0.2 m, and down the pillar the edges come from the centre line and a half-width. The roof rail is a second band of the same material whose first stations lie inside the pillar's top, so it leaves the corner without an end showing, and its lower edge carries on from the pillar's glass-side edge. The headliner sheet's edges end inside the rails (2 cm outside their centre line, 2.2 cm above it) and its front edge 3.5 cm ahead of the header. Each band has 24 points round its section and no crease along its length, which is what removed the faceting. The sun visors are 0.42 m wide (they were 0.50 and ran into the corner).
- **Preview cameras.** `home` is the page's opening view exactly (eye (−0.374, −0.162, 1.207) round the screen centre, 60° tall, 1470×729); `front` stands at (0, −1.3, 1.15), behind the partition with the seat backs and partition hidden, at a 54° field of view, which frames the screen at about the width it has in the rider-guide image; `door` looks in through the open left door like the show-car photos; `corner` looks from between the seats up at the right A-pillar's top.
- **Where the model follows one record over another.** The window buttons and USB-C ports sit under the screen as in the rider-guide image (callouts 7 and 8); a second pair of window switches sits on the console between the seats, where the renders and the issue's seat line put them. The seat back has three blocks (two seams), as in the level-0 wireframe and the show-car photos, not the one seam the reference list above names.
- **Not modelled from the references:** the seat position. The exterior side view puts the seat back roughly 1.9 m behind the cowl, in front of the rear wheel arch; the model's is about 2.2 m behind the windscreen base. The seat itself is not visible there, so this is indirect, and moving the seats would move the eye relative to the screen.
- **The bake of the reshaped cabin** (Kaggle, 2048 px × 256 samples, 130 s from push to COMPLETE): 41,623 faces; Draco GLB 277,764 bytes; WebP lightmaps 238,088 (day, scale 6.25) and 205,660 (night, scale 3.5). The prototype's were 262,616, 175,288 and 160,864; the lightmaps grew with the larger lit surfaces (hood, pillars, headliner front).
- **Drag test after the reshape** (dev build, the procedure under "The Cabin on the page", three runs): the drawn camera stayed within 5.56, 5.50 and 4.99 mm of the controls' camera, the distance to the target between 1.2220 and 1.2281 m, and every fitted limit was reached; a tap zoomed to 0.447 m and Escape returned. The eye box and the orbit were not changed: the new shell stays outside `EYE_BOUNDS` (within the box's y range, up to 0.6, the door cards and the armrest ridge stand at least 0.67 from the centre line against the box's 0.61; the side glass at 0.73 or more; the roof rails and the overhead parts above the box's top at 1.26, or ahead of y 0.6).
- **Trap: the lightmap has to be the second UV set.** The page reads it from UV channel 1. The prototype's Cabin had a first set only because two cup holders came from `primitive_cylinder_add`; built from bmesh parts alone, the joined mesh had just the lightmap set, exported as `TEXCOORD_0`, and `verify.py` failed. The script now gives the Cabin a first set of all-zero coordinates before the lightmap set. Left as `uv_layers.new` fills it (a 0–1 square on every face), it split every vertex and the Draco GLB grew from 277,964 to 1,104,360 bytes, over `verify.py`'s 1 MB limit.
- **Trap: Workbench draws no transparency.** A see-through glass renders as the bare sky colour (seen with a dark glass at alpha 0.6), so in `wb` mode the glass is drawn opaque dark grey.

## The Cabin's colours and light

What #20 changed and what was measured on 2026-11-12. Page readings are from the dev build at 1470×729 and pixel ratio 1, as sRGB values of 255 read back from the canvas; "texel" is the lightmap's light at a point (the value a white surface would show before the page's level), worked out from a frame drawn with white materials, no reflection and no tone mapping.

- **Why the day A-pillars read near-white: the lightmap level, not reflection.** The sunlit side of the left pillar read 231,225,220; with reflection off 228,222,217; with the lightmap off 16,20,18. Its texel was 2.12, 1.95, 1.81 in the sun and 0.93, 1.02, 1.12 in sky light, on a base colour of `#b9bbbd` (0.49 linear) at a level of 0.8: 0.83 linear, which is 235. After the change the same pillars read 167–171.
- **Why everything had a navy cast: blue sky light on dark materials, deepened by the tone mapping.** The bake's sky texture lit the shade blue (dash top texel 0.80, 0.98, 1.17). On a base colour near 0.03 that is about 0.017 linear, and three.js's neutral tone mapping subtracts up to 0.04 from every channel by the smallest one, so a dark colour with a slight tint loses most of its smallest channel: the dash top read 32,36,42 with tone mapping off and 4,15,26 with it on. The reflection probe was not the cause: its far plane (600) did cut off the sky (a sphere of radius 900), but what it held instead was black (the clear colour is `#000000`), and reflection added 3–11 levels anywhere.
- **Trap: three.js adds the environment's diffuse light on top of a lightmap.** `lights_fragment_maps.glsl.js` in 0.187.0 adds `getIBLIrradiance` for a standard material whenever an environment map is set, lightmap or not, and with no occlusion. Once the probe held the blue sky, the floor under the dash read 2,13,27 and the doors 36,41,49, against 0,0,0 and 26,26,26 with the environment off. The Cabin's materials now replace that chunk so the environment gives reflections only (floor 0,3,8, doors 28,32,37 in that test bake); the lightmap is the only diffuse light.
- **The probe** is captured from a seated passenger's eye height (1.2 m) at the cabin's centre (it was captured from the origin, on the road surface) with the cameras' far plane, 1,500 m, which is past the sky.
- **The bake's light** is an even sky of (0.95, 0.97, 1.0) at strength 1, a sun of strength 1.5 spread over 60° high in front, and a road plane of `#a8a8a8` under the car. It was a sky texture with a 3° sun of strength 4, which drew a hard patch across the floor, and no ground, so nothing lit the headliner from below. Night is a dim blue sky (strength 0.05) with the dash strip, the dome lights and the street lamp at the strengths they had; the road plane stays in the night bake, so the lamp's light also comes back up off it, by an amount not measured.
- **Trap: the footwell tub's side walls must stay behind the door cards.** The door cards' lower panel draws in to x = 0.74 − 0.16 ≈ 0.58 m toward the front (`inset`); a tub wall at 0.60 m lay 1–2 cm behind that surface and cut through it, drawing a dark panel with a jagged curved edge on each side of the footwell in the opening view. The walls are at `TUB_X` = 0.65 m, outside every door surface between the floor and the dash underside, so only the tub's rounded front corners show.
- **Trap: the floor under the dash was black in every bake until #20.** The footwell tub is an open shell and `recalc_face_normals` left its floor facing down, so the bake lit the side nobody sees. Its faces are now each turned toward the middle of the recess.
- **The level is 2.7 and the base colours are set for how they read on the page.** Inside the cabin the light differs several times over between surfaces. Worked back from the page readings of the final bake (reading, tone mapping's 0.04 added back, divided by base colour and level): the headliner's texel is 0.23–0.31 (it sees only light bounced off the road and the cabin; in the first 1,024 px test bake, with a darker road, it was about 0.10), the pillars' 0.53, the dash top's 1.0. The level was raised from 0.8 until the pillars read light grey, and each base colour was then set so its surface lands near the reference: headliner `#bcbcbc`, pillars, rails and armrest sweep `#959595`, the dash's front band `#787878`, dash top `#595959`, door cards and the dash's lower layer `#4a4a4a`, leather `#434343`, carpet `#565656`, lever and seat posts `#6c6c6c`, gloss trim `#111111`. They are not measured reflectances: the headliner's is lighter than the pillars' because it gets about half their light, and the dash top's is darker than its front band because it gets twice as much. The hood (`#6f5c3c`, metallic 0.3) is darker than the body's gold for the same reason: it sits in full sky light at a level set for the interior, and reads 131,114,82. The side glass is `#1a1f1e` at alpha 0.25.
- **The dash slab has two materials, split by which side of its profile a face was swept from** (the sides below the vent slot are the lower layer). Split by height instead, the lower layer turned into the upper material toward the slab's ends, where the profile shrinks to 0.3 and rises 2 cm (worked out from the path, not seen in a render).
- **The reflection-only replacement is checked when the Cabin starts**: if three.js's chunk no longer holds the line that is removed, the Cabin does not start and the visitor gets the Reading Home, instead of the cast coming back unnoticed. The replacement also takes away the small multiple-scattering term three.js derives from the same value; its size was not measured. The side glass is not given the replacement and still takes diffuse light from the environment; its pixels were not measured.
- **Colour samples against the references** (page: after #20, day; the seats and the headliner cannot be seen from any point of the orbit, so they were read from a camera placed inside the cabin, drawn by the same renderer). Show-car photos have strong highlights on leather and on the dash pad; the values are medians of a 16 px square at a point chosen off the highlights.

## Baking the cabin in Blender

The cabin is modelled by a Python script run in headless Blender 5.3.0, which also bakes a diffuse lightmap (direct + indirect, no colour) into a second UV set and exports GLB + an HDR lightmap. The web page shows each cabin surface as unlit colour × lightmap, scaled per time of day.

- **Local Mac (M2, Metal, 8 GPU cores): unusable for a first run.** Cycles compiled its Metal kernels for over 24 minutes (two `MTLCompilerService` processes busy, Blender itself idle) without finishing a 1280×720 preview; the run was stopped. The compile is said to be cached after the first run; that was not measured.
- **Kaggle notebook (2 × Tesla T4, OptiX), measured 2026-11-11:** environment setup (download 407 MB Linux Blender, unpack) 29–31 s; 1280×720 preview at 64 samples 11.5–13.8 s; lightmap bake 1024 px × 64 samples 2.9 s, 2048 px × 256 samples 14.2–14.4 s; whole run 62–78 s. Output: GLB 2.6 MB (44,514 faces), HDR lightmap 10.6 MB at 2048 px.
- **Shape iteration runs locally in Workbench.** The Workbench engine needs no Cycles kernels: two 1280×720 previews took 1.2–8.5 s and 0.4–2.6 s on the M2 (the first render of a run is the slow one). Shapes are tuned against the photos there; only the bake goes to Kaggle.
- **Second pass, day + night bake (Kaggle, 2026-11-11):** two 1280×720 Cycles renders 10.4–13.2 s each; day and night lightmaps 2048 px × 256 samples 14.1 s and 14.2 s; whole run 85 s; GLB 2.7 MB (45,191 faces). The night bake lights the cabin only with the dash strip, the dome lights and one warm area light standing in for a street lamp; in the page the night lightmap needed 2.4× intensity to read.
- **Bake noise reads as concrete.** Cycles does not denoise a bake; at 256 samples the speckle on large flat panels made every surface look like cement. Fixed by running OIDN on the baked HDR through the compositor (local, CPU, 0.8–2.4 s per 2048 px map); a side-by-side crop showed the grain gone with shadow edges and panel seams kept.
- **Flat unlit shading also read as concrete.** Drawing the cabin as colour × lightmap (`MeshBasicMaterial`) gave leather, plastic and gloss trim the same chalky look. The cabin now has its own scene with no direct lights, glTF PBR materials (Blender's roughness, as of #20: leather 0.42, dash 0.6, door cards 0.55, pillars 0.65, gloss trim 0.15), the lightmap at intensity × π, and reflections from a PMREM capture of the street, re-captured on phase change. It is drawn over the outside pass with a shared depth buffer (`RenderPass.clear = false`).
- **Trap: the browser kept the old lightmap.** Replacing an asset under the same URL left the noisy one in the browser cache, so the denoised map seemed to change nothing. Version the asset URL whenever an asset is replaced; the committed assets do it with a content hash in the file name (below).
- **Kaggle CLI auth:** the Mac holds the newer OAuth token (`~/.kaggle/credentials.json`). Kaggle CLI 1.7.4 (the newest pip installs on the system Python 3.9) only reads `kaggle.json` and fails; Kaggle CLI 2.2.5 on Python 3.14 reads it, after adding the missing `six` dependency.
- **Trap: a mesh created by `primitive_*_add` stays selected.** Joining "the static parts" with `object.join()` swallowed the screen plane too, and the export then failed with `StructRNA of type Object has been removed`. Deselect all before selecting what to join.
- **Trap: texture orientation for glTF.** The lightmap must load with `flipY = false`; with three.js's default `true` it reads as random dark blotches. The Blender screen plane's UVs also run opposite to a three.js `PlaneGeometry`, so its canvas texture needs `flipY = false` and picking reads `uv.y` unflipped.
- **Trap: clamping the orbiting camera.** Pulling the controls' own camera back inside the cabin feeds into the controls and shrinks the orbit each time it touches a wall, which looks like zooming into the screen during a drag. Draw from a copy (the reference site does the same: `view.copy(camera); keepInRoom(view.position, …)`), and size the orbit limits so the clamp normally never acts: after resizing them, the drawn camera stayed within 5.6 mm (the road sway) of the controls' camera across six full-range drags.

## Compressing the cabin assets

What `tools/cabin/compress.py` writes to `public/cabin/`, measured 2026-11-12 on a fresh bake (Kaggle, 2048 px × 256 samples, whole run 96 s; 113 s from push to downloaded).

- **Sizes.** Raw GLB 2,743,760 bytes → Draco 262,616. Denoised day lightmap 9,678,312 bytes (HDR) → 175,288 (WebP); night 10,226,909 → 160,864. Together 22.6 MB → 599 KB.
- **Lightmap encoding.** Each texel is `light / scale`, sRGB-encoded to 8 bits, in lossy WebP at quality 90; `scale` is the map's brightest value rounded up to a quarter (day 6.0, night 3.0 for that bake; 6.25 and 3.5 for the reshaped cabin of #19) and sits in `manifest.json`. The page loads the texture as sRGB and multiplies the lightmap intensity by `scale`, so no shader change is needed. The baked range is narrow enough for 8 bits: day median 0.71 and maximum 5.9, night median 0.17 and maximum 2.9 (linear, covered texels; read from the 2026-11-11 bake of the same script, as are the format figures below).
- **Formats tried on the day map** (bytes, PSNR on the sRGB-encoded values over covered texels, Blender 5.3.0's encoders): PNG 2,617,786, 59.3 dB (the 8-bit rounding alone); WebP q95 262,340, 46.9 dB; **WebP q90 175,394, 45.2 dB**; WebP q80 109,546, 40.4 dB; AVIF q90 207,448, 47.2 dB; AVIF q80 128,430, 45.1 dB; JPEG q90 484,432, 45.2 dB. The night map gave the same ordering. WebP drops 5 dB between q90 and q80, so the committed maps use q90; AVIF q80 reaches the same PSNR about 47 KB smaller per map. The quality level is a derivation from these figures, not a choice Kihyun made from a side-by-side; #14 keeps that comparison.
- **In the page.** Day and night at the home seat, 1470×729, uncompressed (left) against compressed (right): `home-room-compression-day.webp`, `home-room-compression-night.webp` beside this note. Over an interior-only region (560×130 px of door card and floor) the two differ by a mean of 1.01 of 255 levels by day and 0.70 at night; two loads of the same uncompressed assets differ by 0.32 and 0.24, because the reflected street is placed at random on each load.
- **Geometry.** Draco through Blender's glTF exporter, level 6, 14-bit positions and UVs, 10-bit normals: 262,616 bytes (240,188 gzipped). Meshopt through gltfpack 1.3 at the same bit depths: 527,264 bytes (261,524 gzipped). The decoders, from three.js r169 on jsDelivr: Draco (`draco_decoder.wasm` + `draco_wasm_wrapper.js` + `DRACOLoader.js`) 264,575 bytes, 79,012 gzipped; meshopt (`meshopt_decoder.module.js`) 24,850 bytes, 6,461 gzipped. Geometry plus decoder, if the host serves `.glb` uncompressed and scripts gzipped: Draco 341,628, meshopt 533,725. If the host compresses `.glb` too: Draco 319,200, meshopt 267,985. Whether Vercel compresses `.glb` was not checked. The committed GLB is Draco because it needs no tool beyond Blender and is the smaller one in the case that does not depend on the host; the choice is provisional until #14 measures the first load.
- **Lightmap UV precision.** 14 bits on a 0–1 UV is a quarter of a texel on a 4096 px lightmap; the exporter's default of 12 would be a whole texel there and half a texel at 2048 px.
- **Size limits in `verify.py`.** 1 MB per lightmap is a tenth of the ~10 MB HDR a 2048 px bake produces; 1 MB for the geometry is under half the raw GLB.
- **Trap: gltfpack drops UVs no material uses.** No cabin material references a texture, so by default it removed both UV sets, lightmap UVs included (378,680 bytes, and nothing to map the bake with). `-kv` keeps them.
- **A bake removes the earlier denoised lightmaps.** `compress.py` reads only `light_*_dn.hdr`; with the old ones left in `out/`, skipping the denoise step after a new bake would pair new geometry with the previous bake's light and `verify.py` would still pass. Comparing file times cannot catch it: the files Kaggle hands back carried times nine hours behind the local clock (00:58 for a 09:58 run).
- **Asset names carry a content hash** (`cabin.<8 hex>.glb`), which answers the stale-lightmap trap above without anyone remembering to bump a number; the page reads the names from `manifest.json`.

## Cabin rules

What `src/lib/cabin-rules.ts` decides and why, written with the module on 2026-11-12 (#16). Since #17 the home page's Cabin uses all of it; what was measured in the browser is under "The Cabin on the page" below.

- **The orbit is fitted, not typed in.** `fitOrbit` takes the cabin's inner box and the orbit target and derives the limits in a fixed order: the radius reaches the back wall (max z) less a margin, the polar limits come from the ceiling and the floor at that radius, and the azimuth limits from the side walls and the front wall (min z) at the polar angle that presses hardest against each. It assumes the eye faces the target from +z, which is how the cabin is laid out (the Cabin Screen at the front, the seats behind it).
- **Against the prototype's hand-tuned limits.** For the prototype box (x ±0.61, y 0.82–1.26, z −0.6–0.3) and target (0, 0.84, −0.945) with the 0.02 m margin, the fit gives radius 1.225 m, azimuth ±0.5025 rad, polar 1.2382–1.5708 rad. The prototype had radius 1.18 m, azimuth ±0.5, polar 1.22–1.58. The angles agree to within 0.02 rad; the fitted eye sits 4.5 cm further back. Dragged in a browser on 2026-11-12, the fitted orbit kept the drawn camera within 5.61 mm of the controls' camera (below).
- **The margin is 0.02 m** (`ORBIT_MARGIN`). It has to exceed the road sway added to the drawn camera (5.6 mm in the prototype) so the keep-inside clamp never acts; 0.02 m is a round value above it, and with it the fitted azimuth (±0.5025) lands within 0.003 rad of the prototype's ±0.5; so do 0.01 m (±0.5073) and 0.03 m (±0.4977). A derivation, not a measured optimum.
- **Trap: the limit corners are not the worst case.** The eye is furthest back and furthest sideways where it is level with the target (polar π/2), which can lie between the polar limits rather than at one (it does for an off-centre target at y 1.0: polar 1.3736–1.7018; for the prototype target the upper limit is π/2 itself). Checking only the four limit corners would pass an orbit that leaves the box mid-range, so the tests also walk a grid across the whole range, measured against the bounds less the margin. With the level case removed from the fit, that grid fails for the off-centre target.
- **A refusal means this order ran out of room, not that no orbit exists.** The polar range is taken as wide as the floor and ceiling allow before the front wall is looked at, so a target at y 0.05 under the prototype box is refused at the front wall although an orbit of the same radius with polar 0.3026–0.8699 and azimuth 0 stays inside. Raising the prototype ceiling above about 2.03 m (computed: the eye, straight above the target, then falls short of the front wall) refuses the screen target the same way. A car cabin does not reach that; a taller scene would need the polar range narrowed to the front wall instead.
- **Eye position convention.** Read from three.js source (`Vector3.setFromSphericalCoords`, `Spherical.setFromCartesianCoords`, `OrbitControls.update`, `dev` branch on 2026-11-12): x = r·sin(polar)·sin(azimuth), y = r·cos(polar), z = r·sin(polar)·cos(azimuth); the polar angle is measured from +y and the azimuth around y from +z. `Vector3.setFromSphericalCoords` in the installed three 0.187.0 reads the same.
- **Tap movement is the straight line from press to release**, not the path travelled; a pointer that wanders and returns within 400 ms still counts. Both limits are inclusive (exactly 5 px and exactly 400 ms are taps).
- **The frame test is expressed as "failed"**, so a test still running does not send the visitor to the Reading Home. **The stored choice has three values** (the Cabin, the Reading Home, none): missing WebGL or a failed frame test give the Reading Home whatever was stored, and reduced motion gives it only when nothing was stored, so a visitor who asks for reduced motion and then picks the Cabin gets the Cabin. That last rule is Kihyun's decision (2026-11-12), not a derivation.

## The Cabin on the page

How `src/components/cabin.tsx` and `src/lib/cabin-scene.ts` put the Cabin over the Reading Home (#17), measured on 2026-11-12 on the M2 against `next start` unless a line says otherwise. These were measured in day lighting with the Cabin Screen a blank plane; what #18 added is in the next section.

- **What the server sends.** The home page's server HTML holds no Cabin markup: the component renders nothing on the server and decides at hydration, through `useSyncExternalStore`, from WebGL 2, the two stored values, and the reduced-motion media query. The same decision is taken once more before first paint, for the loading cover only (below). The Cabin is a fixed full-screen layer above the page, portalled into `<body>`; the Reading Home stays in the DOM under it, `inert` while the layer is up (since #22; "Keyboard and screen readers under the Cabin" below).
- **What is stored per browser.** `home` is `cabin` or `reading-home`, written only by the switch. `cabin-frame-test` is `failed`, written when the frame test fails and removed when the visitor picks the Cabin, which is how the test is retried. What the page writes it also keeps in memory and reads from there first, so the switch and a failed test still take effect for the page load in a browser whose localStorage throws; nothing is remembered there. A Cabin that cannot start (no context, an asset that does not load, an orbit that does not fit) is not stored: the visitor gets the Reading Home without the switch until the next page load.
- **three.js is loaded on demand.** The scene module is a dynamic import, so three 0.187.0 reaches only visitors who get the Cabin: one chunk of 672,050 bytes before compression.
- **The Draco decoder needs no copy in `public/`.** `DRACOLoader` in 0.187.0 names its decoder files with `new URL(..., import.meta.url)`, and Turbopack emits them as hashed static files: the glTF decoder is `draco_decoder.wasm` 192,420 bytes and `draco_wasm_wrapper.js` 58,456 bytes, 63,458 and 11,748 over the wire from `next start`. The loader is given `DRACO_GLTF_CONFIG`, the smaller glTF-only pair; a string path would make it look for files next to the page instead.
- **No effect composer.** The street and then the cabin scene are rendered straight into the canvas with `autoClear` off, which is what shares the depth buffer; tone mapping (neutral) and antialiasing come from the renderer. The prototype went through an `EffectComposer` only for the night bloom, which this change does not draw.
- **The side glass** (`Glass` in the GLB) keeps its glTF material (a faint grey tint, alpha 0.25, blended) and gets no lightmap; the scene skips it where it assigns the lightmap to every other mesh but the Cabin Screen.
- **The day light level** is lightmap intensity 2.7 × the manifest's scale × π, reflections at 0.4; where 2.7 comes from is under "The Cabin's colours and light".
- **Zoom moves the drawn camera only.** The orbit's radius is held by `minDistance = maxDistance`, so moving the controls' camera toward the screen would be undone by the next `controls.update()`. The drawn camera is blended from its seat position to a point straight in front of the screen, and returns to where the visitor was looking, not to the opening view. The point's distance makes the screen fill 62% of the view's height (0.447 m at the 60° field of view, the prototype's 0.445 m) or 90% of its width, whichever is further, and never further than the seat; at 390×844 the width decides.
- **Drag test.** Eight drags across the whole viewport (both horizontals, both verticals, four diagonals) on the dev build, sampling every 4 ms, run three times: the drawn camera stayed within 5.61, 5.57 and 5.54 mm of the controls' camera (the road sway alone reaches 5.66 mm, so the keep-inside clamp never acted) and its distance to the target stayed between 1.2221 and 1.2281 m around the 1.225 m radius. The last two runs also read the angles: the drags reached every fitted limit (azimuth ±0.5025, polar 1.2382 and 1.5708). The tab drew 6, 12 and 4 frames a second while these ran (see "Measuring in the automation browser"), so the samples cover 591, 709 and 459 drawn frames. A drag that ends over the screen did not zoom; a drag while zoomed left both angles unchanged, and the zoomed camera sat 0.447 m from the screen.
- **A frame's cost is timed, not the gap between frames.** The frame test and the resolution steps both time one frame with a one-pixel `readPixels` after it, which makes the GPU finish. Gaps between `requestAnimationFrame` calls measure the display and the tab as much as the scene: this automation tab calls back 4–6 times a second, and a 50 Hz display would sit exactly on a 20 ms threshold.
- **The thresholds are derivations, not measurements.** At each pixel ratio (1.5, 1.25, 1, capped at the device's) the first 3 frames are skipped and the next 12 timed; a median over 20 ms (the prototype's step-down figure) steps down, and at the lowest step a median over 33.3 ms (30 frames a second, the lower of the two frame budgets #14 still has to choose between) fails the test. The loading state stays up until the test passes. Afterwards one frame in 30 is timed and five such timings decide a step down; the resolution never steps back up. No slow device was tried: the failing path was exercised only by setting both thresholds to 0.0001 ms, which stepped 1.5 → 1 and gave the Reading Home within a second, kept it after a reload, and ran the test again when the Cabin was picked.
- **Loading, locally (#17).** Over three reloads the Cabin's layer was in the page at 72–105 ms and the loading state ended at 590–661 ms; first contentful paint was at 128–144 ms. That compared DOM presence with a paint and did not show what was on screen; the loading cover below replaced the conclusion drawn from it.
- **The Reading Home showed before the Cabin, for two reasons** (measured 2026-11-12, #21, production build of c0677db, headless Chrome for Testing 1228 on ANGLE Metal, a performance trace with a screenshot of every frame). The layer is added at hydration, after first paint (warm load: first paint 136 ms, layer 144 ms). And the layer was a direct child of `<main>`, so the page-load entrance rule in `globals.css` (`main > *`, opacity 0 → 1 over 0.6 s) faded it in: the frames showed the Reading Home, with the Cabin's switch already drawn, until 480 ms and then a gradual darkening to the loading state at about 800 ms; cold loads showed the same. The layer and the switch now go into `<body>` through a portal, outside the rule.
- **The loading cover.** A blocking `<head>` script (`coverScript` in `src/lib/cabin-cover.ts`, after the theme script) asks `homeFor` itself, serialised into the script with `Function.prototype.toString` (the way next-themes serialises its script), with what is known before paint: WebGL 2, the two stored values, the reduced-motion query. On `/` and when it says Cabin, it sets `data-cabin="cover"` on `<html>`, and `globals.css` draws a `body::before` in the Cabin's loading look (the `cabin-loading` utility, shared with the stage's status line). Its text has empty alternative text (`content: '…' / ''`), so screen readers keep reading the Reading Home; a plain `content` line before it serves browsers without that syntax, which would otherwise drop the declaration and the cover with it. The component lifts the cover when the Cabin is ready, at once when it decides on the Reading Home, the frame test fails or the Cabin cannot start, and when it unmounts (a keyboard user can reach the links under the cover and leave `/` by client-side navigation; without the last, the next page stayed covered and the Cabin was off when they came back). The production build serialises the minified `homeFor` and timer function, which need nothing outside themselves. In a dev build, React's strict mode runs the unmount once at hydration, so there the cover lifts as the stage mounts (the stage looks the same) and the give-up never fires; try the limit on a production build.
- **The limit is 5 s of the page being visible, counted from the start of the load** (`COVER_LIMIT_MS`), a derivation from these readiness times, not a figure Kihyun chose: fresh browser, cache disabled, local server 1.77–3.18 s (three runs); throttled to 40 ms / 20 Mbit/s 2.25 s, 150 ms / 9 Mbit/s 3.25 s, 150 ms / 1.6 Mbit/s 8.21 s (one run each). At 1.6 Mbit/s the visitor gets the Reading Home after 5 s of the cover. Time while the page is hidden does not count: a hidden tab gets no animation frames, so the frame test cannot finish there, and nobody is looking at its cover. The `<head>` script holds the only timer; at the limit it turns the mark into `gave-up` (which lifts the cover even when no page script runs) and dispatches `cabin-cover-gave-up`, on which the component shows the Reading Home with the switch to the Cabin (Kihyun's call, 2026-11-12). Pressing the switch mounts the stage again without the cover. A component that hydrates after the give-up reads the mark instead. A stage mounted without the cover (the switch, a client-side navigation to `/`) has no limit, as before #21.
- **What the cover measured** (#21, production build, headless Chrome for Testing 1228 on ANGLE Metal, a performance trace with a screenshot of every frame). Cold load, three runs: first paint 268–304 ms, the first frame of the page is the cover, the cover ends when the Cabin is ready at 1866–2096 ms; no frame of the Reading Home. Reload, three runs: the same, first paint 212–220 ms, ready 1266–1283 ms. (c0677db on reload: Reading Home frames and the fade, as above.) No mark and no cover frame for a stored Reading Home choice, reduced motion with no choice, a stored failed frame test, WebGL off (`--disable-3d-apis`, with `cabin` stored), or `/projects`; reduced motion with `cabin` stored gets the cover and the Cabin. The cover lifted with the Reading Home on top: GLB and lightmaps blocked 388 ms, the scene chunk failing 285 ms, every JS chunk blocked about 5,036 ms (the first frame without it; the `<head>` timer alone), the assets never answering 5,018 ms (on c0677db that last case stayed on the loading state). A background tab, simulated (`document.hidden` true and `requestAnimationFrame` held, timers running; headless Chrome reports background tabs as visible): still covered after 9.7 s hidden, the Cabin ready with its switch within 3 s of being shown. Keyboard: Tab to "See all" while covered (assets held) and Enter: `/open-source` uncovered; back through the wordmark, the Cabin with its switch; a build without the unmount lift left `/open-source` covered and the Cabin off. The switch both ways, with reloads and a stored failed frame test, behaved as after #17. The drag test above, re-run three times on the dev build with the layer in `<body>`: gap at most 4.56 mm each run, distance 1.2225–1.2271 m, every limit reached; the tap (0.447 m), Escape, the drag onto the screen and the drag while zoomed behaved as before.
- **The `<head>` script's WebGL check costs a context before first paint:** 3.9–7.3 ms for the first WebGL 2 context in a fresh browser, 2.0–3.3 ms for the next (five fresh browsers, M2). The script first asks `homeFor` with WebGL assumed and creates the context only when the stored values and the reduced-motion query leave the Cabin possible, so a stored Reading Home choice, a stored failed frame test and reduced motion with no choice cost none.
- **Trap: anything placed in `<main>` gets the entrance animation.** `main > *` in `globals.css` fades and slides every direct child of `<main>` in over 0.6 s, which is how the Cabin's full-screen layer came to fade in over the Reading Home. A page-level overlay goes into `<body>` instead.
- **Trap: `next dev` on `127.0.0.1` does not hydrate.** Next 16 blocks its dev resources for a host not in `allowedDevOrigins`, so the page stays the server HTML and the Cabin never mounts. Use `localhost`.
- **Trap: `OrbitControls` sets the canvas cursor.** In 0.187.0, with `cursorStyle = 'grab'`, it writes `grabbing` on pointer down and `grab` on pointer up, from a listener on the document that runs after the canvas's own and does not look at `enabled`. A cursor set from a canvas listener is overwritten, so the zoomed state switches `cursorStyle` to `auto` instead.
- **`renderer.dispose()` does not release the WebGL context** in 0.187.0; the scene calls `forceContextLoss()` first, so switching between the Cabin and the Reading Home does not pile up contexts. A context lost while the Cabin runs is not handled: the layer would stay up, drawing nothing, until the visitor uses the switch. Not tried.
- **Reading the cameras.** In a dev build the mounted scene is on `window.__cabin`; its `probe()` returns the gap between the two cameras, the drawn camera's distance to the target, the orbit angles and the pixel ratio.

## Keyboard and screen readers under the Cabin

What #22 changed in `src/components/cabin.tsx`, `src/components/home-nav.tsx`, `src/lib/home.ts` and the scene, checked on 2026-11-12 on `next start` in the ego-browser tab at 1470×729, with Tab pressed through CDP input and each stop's centre hit-tested with `elementFromPoint`.

The effect's cleanup takes it off, so the Reading Home comes back whole whenever the stage goes: the switch, a failed frame test, a Cabin that cannot start, the 5 s give-up, leaving `/`. `inert` and not `visibility: hidden` or `aria-hidden`: it takes the elements out of the tab order and the accessibility tree in one attribute and leaves what is drawn under the opaque layer alone. In the Reading Home after the switch, Tab reaches every Section link and both "See all". While loading (the Cabin's assets held unanswered): only the switch; at the give-up, nothing inert and Tab reaches the Reading Home's links. ## Measuring in the automation browser

- **Frame rate cannot be measured there.** The ego-browser tab ran `requestAnimationFrame` at 4 per second even with nothing to draw (counted over 1.5 s, tab visible and focused). Every fps figure read in that tab measured the throttle, not the scene. Frame time right after a scripted click or drag also dropped to 1–11 fps and recovered.
- **What works instead:** render one frame, force the GPU to finish with a one-pixel `readPixels`, time it, repeat ~40–70 times, take the median, and subtract the same timing for an empty scene. The empty-scene floor was 52 ms in one session and about 0.5 ms in later ones, so always measure the floor in the same run. Results still varied run to run by up to ±3 ms, and once from 5.3 to 12.3 ms within three runs: treat single figures as rough.

## Traps

- **Turning in place freezes the window.** With the eye fixed and only rotation, the window is a fixed aperture: it shows the same slice of the outside from every angle. The prototypes moved the eye at most 18 cm, so the outside read as a poster. The reference orbits instead (above).
- **The pixel-ratio governor must ignore deliberately spaced frames.** Idle backdrop frames are 33 ms apart on purpose; fed into a "frames are slow" average they step the resolution down for nothing. Only back-to-back frames during movement say anything about speed.
- **Update the camera's world matrix before projecting labels or picking.** Setting `camera.rotation` does not update `matrixWorld` until the next render; projecting a label right after placed it at the previous frame's position (seen as a tag at (308, 65) instead of over the model).
- **`all: unset` after positioning properties wipes them.** It must come first in a rule.
- **AgX tone mapping flattens a photograph.** The living-photo room switched to neutral tone mapping to keep the photo's colours.
- **transformers.js `toCanvas()` returns an `OffscreenCanvas`** (v3.0.2), which has no `toDataURL`; draw it into a regular canvas first.
- **Wikimedia's API rate-limits bursts.** About 20 search requests in quick succession were refused ("You are making too many requests"). Space requests or use the dumps.

## Assets tried

- **Polyfork** (polyfork.dev): 3,205 low-poly GLB models; free mesh files under CC BY 4.0 (credit Polyfork, say if changed) or the Polyfork licence (no credit, no redistribution). A site serving the GLB files redistributes them, so CC BY applies. Served from its CDN with `access-control-allow-origin: *`; the desk monitor GLB is 36,936 bytes. The Open Plan kit has 63 room parts on one palette and scale. No Tesla or other EV model in the catalogue (searched `tesla`, `cyber`, `electric`).
- **Window photograph:** "Sobaeksan as seen from Woraksan Yeongbong Peak (7)", Wikimedia Commons, CC0, 3648×2056. Shot from a summit, so its horizon does not match a seated room's eye height; a fitted camera or a photo taken nearer eye height is still needed.
- **Depth map:** Depth Anything V2 small (`onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small`) through transformers.js 3.0.2 in the browser: 1280×721 in about 7 s on the M2. Output is near-bright, far-dark, the same convention as the reference's `depth.png`.

## Why the prototypes read as fake

An analysis, not a measurement, kept here as the checklist for the Blender pass: colour-only procedural textures with no normal, roughness or AO maps; no bounced or baked light, so every surface is evenly lit; razor-sharp, perfectly regular geometry; a photograph whose viewpoint does not match the room, on a flat card with no fitted camera; no brightness gap between the bright outside and the darker inside; night made by grading a day photograph.
